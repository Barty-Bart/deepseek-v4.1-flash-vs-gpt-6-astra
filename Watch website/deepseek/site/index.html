    The craft

    A mechanical watch is a small argument for doing things properly.

    The M01 case is turned from a single block of stainless steel, then brushed in one direction across the flanks and polished on the bezel so the two finishes meet on a crisp line.

    Inside, an automatic movement winds itself from the motion of your wrist and keeps a 40-hour reserve — enough to take it off on Friday evening and find it running on Monday morning.

    The dial is finished with a fine sunray brush that runs outward from the centre, which is why the M01 changes character as you move. Applied indices sit slightly proud of the surface so the hands read clearly at a glance.

    The bracelet is built from flat three-link sections that taper toward the clasp, with a concealed folding clasp so the outside stays clean.

    Our story

    Founded in 2018 on a simple disagreement.

    MERIDIAN began in 2018 with a small argument about what an everyday mechanical watch should cost.

    Four people — an engineer, a designer, a machinist and someone who had spent a decade servicing watches for other people — spent two years on the M01 case before the first one was assembled.

    The studio is deliberately narrow in scope. One case architecture, a handful of dials, and a bracelet that is finished to the same standard on the inside as the outside. Nothing is added for the sake of a feature list.

    Eight years later the collection is still built around that first case. Watches have found 9,800 owners. Around 12,400 have been sold since the studio opened.
